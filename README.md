The AI side of the NovaTrail tutor: it reads the learner's work, handwriting or text, into an observation, reviews the working for the first mistake, assesses an essay against the module author's rubric, and words the hint for the rung of the hint ladder that the platform's program chose. It holds the platform's prompts, one per AI capability and each with its own eval set, the adapters to AI providers, the exact models admitted for each capability, and the one function that admits an AI route for the learner's age category before every request; it reports each call's usage, by which api counts the platform AI quotas, and its eval runner is a CLI on the same code. It is a library inside api: it decides no verdict, picks no lesson or task and stores nothing, and its requests carry no identifier of the learner or the account: on the own-key route only the key itself goes along, to its own provider, which ties each call to the key owner's account.

A component of [NovaTrail](https://github.com/altnovatrail/spec/tree/v0.16.7), a universal open platform for adaptive learning, interactive lessons and AI tutoring. It belongs to stage 2, First slice with learners, with canvas, api, app, skill-map and infra; together with canvas it brings the stage's first code, which for tutor is the check board below.

## Status

The MVP is the check board: you solve eleven fixed maths tasks by hand in the browser and press Check at any step, before you finish too. Five are solved in lines; the other six, tasks 6 to 11, in free form: on a number line, with a drawing, or with the working at the side. The Claude models you tick read the work, each at the effort you choose, and Claude Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 without thinking if you like. Each gives only a verdict: correct, no mistakes so far, an error, which the model marks on the board in its own colour, or cannot read. You mark whether each was right. Each answer shows its time, when its first token came, its price and its tokens, thinking included; the summary shows, for each model at each effort, how often it was right and what an answer costs and takes. On 2026-10-23 Claude Opus 5.5 at high effort judged all five tasks the board then had right, at about a cent and five seconds a check (R20 in [docs/decisions.md](docs/decisions.md)); GPT-6 Astra, which the board can also ask, is not used. The reading measurement of epic E2 asked one question: does the writing process, stroke order and erasures, help Claude judge a finished sheet? On 40 sheets, 39 of them drawn by code with the solution in lines from the top down, it did not show any help: Claude Opus 5.5 judged every sheet right from the picture alone (R27). Epic E4 (R28) came next and was done on 2026-10-29: solutions in free form, with a number line, a drawing or notes at the side, then reading the work into an observation with and without the key, hints, workbook cells and essays. It started with a pilot on this board, and spec recorded its conclusions in v0.3.2 (R49 in [docs/decisions.md](docs/decisions.md)). Its extended measurement followed in the same epic (R50): more Claude configurations, hundreds of sheets, pages and essays that code draws, and ten sheets the author wrote messily; spec recorded what it showed in v0.3.3 (R51). The epic ended with the workbook reading under that tag's rule for a digit written mirrored: such a digit is not read as a digit, the box counts as ambiguous, and the learner writes it again. Start the board in a terminal of the devcontainer:

```
just board
```

It asks for the Anthropic and OpenAI keys for eval without showing them, so no key lands in the shell's history; Enter skips a model. When the terminal says the board is at http://127.0.0.1:8080, open that address, or the one the Ports tab of VS Code shows if it forwarded the port under another number. Until then the page only keeps loading: VS Code forwards the port, but nothing answers on it yet.

The measurement's sheets are written in the board's record mode, which asks no model and so needs no key:

```
just record
```

Pick a sheet of the plan, write what it shows with a pen or a mouse, press Done, check the markup the plan filled in and press Save. If the board grouped the strokes into lines wrongly, pick strokes on the sheet and move them to another line, or join two lines. The board checks the markup after every change, and its words show beside the fields. On a sheet with a planned erasure, the board takes the move of the eraser on the plan's line for it, and you check that too. The sheet is saved as a sample of spec's eval protocol, its history of writing — every stroke's points with their time and the pointer that wrote it, every erasure, Undo and Clear — in `samples/sheets-v1`, outside git, once spec's `sample-check` passes it. Undo and Clear are slips; Start over writes the sheet anew. A stroke in no line keeps the sheet from being saved until you move it to one or take it away. Your writer's code, which every sample of yours names, is made at random at the first save, in `samples/writer-code`. The sheets saved before as files of tutor-sample v0 are converted beside their directories by `go run ./cmd/tutor-eval convert -from DIR`, which leaves the old files as they are.

The three free-form sheets of epic E4 that you write yourself are made of parts: a number line, a drawing, notes at the side. `just free-record` starts the same mode on them and saves them in `samples/free-v1`. After Done the board frames each group of strokes as a part, in the order you wrote them. A drawing you drew on after a pause comes in two frames, so join them; to move strokes to another part, drag a rectangle around them to pick them.

The sheets you do not write, code draws: `go run ./cmd/tutor-eval draw` draws every sheet of the plan that has no file yet, saves it beside yours with the writer `synthetic`, and leaves yours as they are. With `-png DIR` it also saves, in DIR, the final picture of each sheet it draws and its frame before each erasure. For epic E4, code draws into `synthetic/`, out of git too. No handwriting is in it, so coding agents work with it and run the measurements on it themselves, and the board never writes there: your handwriting stays in `samples/`.

Once the sheets are written and split into a development and a test part (`tutor-eval sheets`, then `tutor-eval split` once), the measurement runs on a part:

```
just measure -part dev
```

The measurement of free writing of epic E4 runs the same way on the 40 sheets code draws, `just free -part dev`, in the judge's prompt for free writing; `-part all` runs every sheet of a plan without the split, as for your three sheets. Either run first says how many calls it makes and what they are expected to cost, and starts only when you type yes. It starts no call that could take it past its cap, $3 for the development part, $6 for the test and $2 for all the sheets, and logs every answer in `runs` beside the folder of the sheets, outside git. With `-stub` it asks no model: a stub answers by the markup, and the run needs no key. For the extended measurement, `go run ./cmd/tutor-eval gen sheets|near` makes a bank of tasks and a plan of their sheets with their truth by construction, as new files, and `gen workbook|essays` the pages of the workbook or the essays, and `draw -shift` leans the answer's digit of a near sheet as far as it says. `run -kind essay -two` assesses an essay in two calls, reading it and then assessing the text read. `-readers` names the Claude configurations a run asks: the three of E4 by default, and for the extended measurement four more, Opus 5.5 at medium, Sonnet 5.5 at low with thinking and without, and Haiku 5.5 at medium (R50). For it you write ten more sheets in free form, messily on purpose, with `just messy-record`: they go to `samples/free-messy`, out of git. `go run ./cmd/tutor-eval report LOG` then turns the log into the report, asking no model: whether the writing process helps each judge, by the criterion of section 1.1 of [docs/measurements.md](docs/measurements.md), and the other numbers, in Markdown and CSV beside the log.

The workbook of epic E4 is 20 pages of `eval/workbook.json`: ten items a page, each with its boxes, and in each box a value written by hand, plainly or with a digit leaning toward its pair, mirrored or crossed out and written again. Its pages are listed in [docs/workbook.md](docs/workbook.md). `go run ./cmd/tutor-eval draw -workbook eval/workbook.json` draws them as samples of spec's eval protocol into `synthetic/workbook`, which `tutor-eval workbook` and the runs read, and with `-png synthetic/png/workbook` it saves the crop of every filled box it draws, what a reading sees. The essays of epic E4 are 20 short essays in English with one rubric of six criteria, and whether each essay meets each criterion as planned: `eval/essays.json`, listed in [docs/essays.md](docs/essays.md). `go run ./cmd/tutor-eval draw -essays eval/essays.json` draws them into `synthetic/essays`, and with `-png synthetic/png/essays` saves the crop of every page it draws.

api makes the library once, when it starts, with `tutor.New`: spec's admission table, the facts of its deployment, the least stage of admission its learners take, the platform's keys at the AI providers and the adapters of the providers it calls, `claude.Dial` of `provider/claude` and `gpt.Dial` of `provider/gpt`. The library builds no adapter in, so api's build carries the SDK of only the providers it takes (R90 in [docs/decisions.md](docs/decisions.md)). Each call carries a `tutor.Route` — the AI route, the learner's age category and, on the own key, the key, which never prints — and ends in a `tutor.Outcome` with a record of usage for each call of a model; `example_test.go` shows it. `Words` asks the admitted model for the analysis of the steps and the tutor's question of a rung in one call, and without the work for the same rung put another way: the box of the first mistake, the trap and the question. Package `words` holds the checks api makes of the tutor's words beside the leak check: `words.Hint`, the form of a hint, and `words.Feedback`, the rule of feedback by a rubric, against the text read (R70 of spec). `ReadSheet` reads a handwriting sheet — the PNG of its crop and the open view of its task — by the admitted pair of readers, in parallel, and gives the observation of each, which module-kit joins into the verdict, or the outcome alone.

A model serves a capability only once tutor's rule of admission passes it on a runner's report (R80 in [docs/decisions.md](docs/decisions.md)). The job of `.github/workflows/eval.yml` reads the platform's key for eval from Secret Manager of infra, in the GitHub environment `eval`; for now it is started by hand and only checks the way in, at no cost (R120 spec). For the first app the set is your own sheets: `just admit-record` opens the record mode on the 54 sheets of `eval/author-sheets.json`, into `samples/author-v1`, out of git. `go run ./cmd/tutor-eval run -kind reading -reads plain -part recorded` reads the sheets recorded so far as the platform does, every reader in every repeat, and `go run ./cmd/tutor-eval admit REPORT` says which stage the report admits its readers to, full or author, with the numbers it rests on.

The tutor's words have an eval set of their own, with points on the learner's sex and on safety (R85 in [docs/decisions.md](docs/decisions.md)): the sheets with a mistake of `eval/lines-gen-1.json`, ten of them again with a note at the side that is no part of the solution and not for a child, and 330 cases of the words on them — a rung with the work, the rung put another way without it, and a sheet with a note — in `eval/words-sheets.json` and `eval/words-cases.json`, which `go run ./cmd/tutor-eval gen words -seed 118` makes. `draw` draws the sheets into `synthetic/words-v1`, `run -kind hint -part all -cases eval/words-cases.json` asks the cases, and `go run ./cmd/tutor-eval judge-words -log LOG` has a judge model read every hint: whether it tells the learner's sex, keeps to the task, suits a child and takes the note up. The judge counts only once `judge-words -controls eval/words-judge.json` shows it passing all its control phrases; Sonnet 5.5 at low judges by default (R86). On your own sheets with a mistake, `go run ./cmd/tutor-eval gen words-cases` makes the cases of a written plan, and `run -kind hint -part recorded -cases F -case-kinds rung` asks those of the sheets recorded so far. `go run ./cmd/tutor-eval admit-words -controls LOG HINT-LOG...` applies the rule of admission of the words: the stage author, or every reason why not. Its first run, on 2026-11-08, did not admit Opus 5.5 at high (R86), and the words wait for task T127.

After a correct answer, `CorrectAnswerWords` asks the admitted model for a few words: the praise of a step of the solution and one question on the technique that helped, with the work or, for a task of choices alone, without it. `words.Correct` holds their form — exactly one question, and it the last sentence, at most two sentences — and api gives no words that fail it or the leak check (R112 in [docs/decisions.md](docs/decisions.md)). Their eval set is synthetic: `go run ./cmd/tutor-eval gen correct -seed 143` makes `eval/correct-sheets.json` and its 110 cases, `eval/correct-cases.json`, `draw` draws the sheets into `synthetic/correct-v1`, `run -kind correct -part all -cases eval/correct-cases.json` asks them, `judge-words -log LOG` judges the words, and `admit-words` applies their rule of admission to these logs. Task T144 admitted Opus 5.5 at high to them on 2026-11-14, at the stage "author" (R113 in [docs/decisions.md](docs/decisions.md)).

Canvas's sample page and tutor's runner must read the same files. `just canvas-check` downloads the synthetic samples of the release of canvas that the `justfile` pins, by its tag and the SHA-256 of the archive, into `synthetic/`, and runs `tutor-eval canvas` on them: each sample is held to spec's rules, read by the task it names, with the bank at the tag of tutor it names, and run on the stub, which asks no model, with the runner's report of the whole set. It fails on a sample the runner cannot read or refuses but as a child's, and `just canvas-check-test` makes sure it does; CI runs both.

How it works is section 3.3 of [docs/architecture.md](docs/architecture.md), and the measurements of epics E2 and E4 are [docs/measurements.md](docs/measurements.md); what the library is to be, call by call, is sections 3.5 to 3.10. ## Releases

A release comes out by itself after each merge into `main` that changes what the module is built from: its Go code, the files that code embeds, `go.mod`, `go.sum`, the `justfile`, the license, the Dockerfile, the release workflow or the file of open views below (R62 in [docs/decisions.md](docs/decisions.md)). Its number is the next patch of line 0.1, from v0.1.0. A release is the tag of the Go module, which api builds from, `go get github.com/altnovatrail/tutor@v0.1.0`, and its files: the source of that commit, `tutor_<version>_source.tar.gz`; from the first release after 2026-11-02, the open views of the measurement's tasks, `tutor_<version>_open-tasks.json`; and `SHA256SUMS` over them. The open views are what a learner sees of each of the 56 tasks of the measurements — its statement, its canvas and its answer slots, in spec's module format 0.2 up to v0.1.1 and 0.3 from the first release after task T30 of [RUN.md](RUN.md) — and none of its key, solution, traps or checks: until the modules repository has content, canvas's CI puts them into the build of its sample page, by the release's tag and the file's sum (R64). The file is `eval/open-tasks.json`, and `just open-tasks` writes it anew from the banks. Each file comes with its Sigstore bundle, `<file>.sigstore.json`. The releases are immutable, and `gh release verify <tag> -R altnovatrail/tutor` and `gh release verify-asset <tag> <file> -R altnovatrail/tutor` check a release and a file you downloaded. The Go command checks the module itself against the Go checksum database.
